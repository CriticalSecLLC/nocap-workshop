The Steering Committee approves the sessions and leads at the No-CAP Workshop and resolves situations that arise between leads, sponsors, and attendees. This page is its roster and its charter, and it will be amended as we proceed through the workshop planning and execution process.

## Members

{{< committee >}}

The committee is formed: all three voting seats are filled. Sessions and leads are approved as proposals are reviewed, and anything on the schedule before then is a placeholder.

## Who Serves

Members serve as individuals, not as representatives of their employers. We look for:

- Practitioner experience: people who have done this work, who know what it's like to have skin in the game.
- Capacity to be neutral: able to weigh a session on its merits when it involves their employer, their clients, or their competitors.
- Broad perspective across FedRAMP, security, and engineering. This is strongly preferred.

To volunteer or nominate someone, [write to us](mailto:questions@nocap-workshop.com?subject=Steering%20Committee).

## Purpose

The committee exists so that what is presented at the workshop is chosen on merit by practitioners, and so that problems between leads, sponsors, and attendees have a fair, neutral place to go.

## Authority

The committee decides which sessions, tracks, and leads are approved, and it resolves disputes about content and conduct among leads, sponsors, and attendees.

The organizer, CriticalSec LLC, convenes the committee and handles intake, scheduling, the platform, and all commercial matters, including sponsor agreements, invoices, and refunds. The committee does not set prices or negotiate sponsorships. The organizer may act immediately on a safety, legal, or platform matter and will report the action to the committee. The organizer may delegate these duties to staff or contractors, who act under the organizer's direction.

## Membership

- There are three voting seats.
- The organizer holds one seat and may choose to step back from it later.
- New members are invited by the sitting members, with their affiliations disclosed, and confirmed by a majority vote of the voting members.
- Members serve through the January 2027 workshop and are re-confirmed each year after that.
- A member may resign at any time, and may be removed by a majority vote of the other members for violating this charter.

## Neutrality

- Sessions, leads, tracks, scope, seats, and committee decisions are settled on their merits, independent of sponsorship. Deliberations stay within the committee.
- Vendors are treated on equal terms. Any vendor may propose a session or a multi-day track, and every proposal is judged against the same published criteria.
- Nothing about the workshop implies an endorsement of a product, a company, or an approach by the committee, the organizer, FedRAMP, or any agency.

## Conflicts of Interest

- Members disclose their affiliations and employers when they join, and update them when they change. Members are never asked to name their clients.
- A member recuses from any decision involving their employer, a client, or a direct competitor of either, including any session they would lead themselves. A member with a client conflict says only that a conflict exists and keeps the client's identity confidential. A recused member does not vote and does not take part in the discussion of that item.
- The organizer's own sessions and private session listings are decided by the other members.

## Approving Sessions and Leads

A session or track is approved when it is:

- Led by practitioners who have done the work they describe.
- Concrete and demonstrable: the tooling, the tradeoffs, the numbers, and real examples.
- Relevant to VDR: it helps teams meet FedRAMP's VDR requirements and fits the workshop's three days.
- Focused on the work. Showing a product doing the work is welcome, and it is how teams come to understand the value of what is being demonstrated.
- Accurate and fair, including where it involves competing approaches.
- Safe to present: it uses sanitized or synthetic data and keeps non-public vulnerabilities and non-public government information confidential.
- For a multi-day track, built around a defined deliverable for attendees on each day.
- Led by someone who agrees to the conduct expectations below and to the session being recorded and posted publicly.

Proposals come in through the [proposal form](/propose/). The organizer checks them for completeness and passes them to the committee. The committee approves or declines, and the organizer tells the proposer. A decline comes with a short reason on request, and a proposal may be resubmitted. The criteria are described in more detail on the [Lead Agreement](/lead-agreement/#evaluation-criteria) page.

## Approving Private Session Listings

Private sessions are booked and hosted directly between a provider and a team. The committee decides who may be listed on the [Private Sessions](/private-sessions/) page, in the same vote as the proposal. A provider is listed when:

- Their public session or track at the workshop is approved. Listings are tied to a public session or track, and a listing ends if the public session is declined or withdrawn.
- Their public session is useful on its own, and the private offering gives them an opportunity to meet with teams on their terms and discuss how they can support their specific needs.
- The description is honest and realistic about outcomes, and it stands on its own: FedRAMP, agencies, the committee, and the workshop remain independent of the provider. Pricing is described as tailored to the team.
- The provider has disclosed any affiliation or financial interest the committee should know about.
- The provider agrees to the [private session requirements](/lead-agreement/#private-session-listings) in the Lead Agreement.

A listing stays for at least six months after the workshop. After that the committee will decide whether to continue hosting the site and may choose to review existing listings. The committee may remove a listing sooner for a breach of the Lead Agreement or this charter, or for an undisclosed conflict, through the process under Disputes. A listing is a directory entry, and each provider sets their own prices and stands behind their own results. Listings are decided on these criteria, separately from sponsorship.

## Voting

- A decision needs approval from a majority of the voting members who are not recused, and never fewer than two approving votes.
- A tie means not approved.
- At least two voting members must take part in any decision.
- Proposals are decided within five business days of the proposal deadline, and the organizer tells each lead the outcome.

## Disputes

A lead, sponsor, or attendee with a concern about content, conduct, or fairness involving a lead, sponsor, or attendee may [report it to us](mailto:questions@nocap-workshop.com?subject=Steering%20Committee%20concern).

- We acknowledge a report within three business days and decide within ten, unless more time is needed and the people involved are told.
- Members involved in or connected to the matter recuse.
- Outcomes include guidance, a warning, changes to or removal of a session, removal of a lead or attendee from the event, and withdrawal of sponsor benefits. Refunds and credits are handled by the organizer under the sponsor terms.
- A person may ask for reconsideration once, within seven days, with new information. Where possible it is decided by members who were not involved in the first decision.
- Commercial disputes about pricing, invoices, or refunds go to the organizer and not to the committee.

## Conduct

Everyone taking part is expected to be professional and respectful, to give credit where it is due, and to leave other people's confidential information alone. Harassment, discrimination, and disruption are not acceptable. Everyone keeps it positive: focus on what you bring, and speak respectfully of other products, vendors, services, and customers. A lead who sets this aside is removed from the session, and the session is not posted. See the [Positivity Rule](/lead-agreement/#what-leads-agree-to). Concerns go through the dispute process above, and the organizer may remove a disruptive person from a live session immediately.

## Confidentiality

Committee deliberations and the details of proposals stay private. Sessions must not disclose non-public vulnerabilities in anyone's environment or non-public government information.

## Competition Law

The workshop brings competitors together. Everyone taking part, including leads, committee members, sponsors, attendees, and the organizer, must not agree on or exchange information about prices, bids, customers, or markets, whether in a session or in any conversation connected to the workshop. Non-competitive discussions of technical compliance practices that support effective security for products and the protection of customers are permitted, provided they do not involve competitively sensitive business information. Anyone who sees a discussion drifting toward the prohibited topics should say so right away to the committee, and a lead or the organizer may stop it on the spot. Violations are handled under Disputes.

For background, see the [DOJ and FTC Antitrust Policy Statement on Sharing of Cybersecurity Information](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/atr/legacy/2014/04/10/305027.pdf) and the [Linux Foundation Antitrust Policy](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/legal/antitrust-policy), both listed under Sources below.

## Government Participants

Government employees take part under their own agencies' ethics and conflict-of-interest rules. Their participation is not an endorsement, and the workshop is independent of FedRAMP and the U.S. government.

## Transparency

This charter, the roster, and the approval criteria are public, and the approved sessions and tracks appear on the schedule. Deliberations stay private.

## Amendments

The voting members may amend this charter by majority vote. Every change is tracked, with its date, in the [page's change history on GitHub](https://github.com/CriticalSecLLC/nocap-workshop/commits/main/content/steering.md), and takes effect when it is published here.

## Rampart Track

Rampart is the organizer's founding track. It is on the schedule as a placeholder and was not selected by the committee. It will be assessed by the committee, against the same criteria as every other track, once the committee is formed.

## Sources

This process was adapted from published practices in other communities. None of these organizations has reviewed or endorsed this charter, and the charter is simpler than any of them.

- [CNCF Charter, section 6 (Technical Oversight Committee)](https://github.com/cncf/foundation/blob/main/charter.md): a standing committee that decides by majority vote, expects its members to weigh the community's goals against corporate interests, and can remove members by vote.
- [USENIX Security Symposium submission policy on conflicts of interest (2015)](https://www.usenix.org/node/188557): committee members with a conflict, chairs included, are excluded from evaluating and discussing the submission. Our rule for the organizer's own sessions follows the same idea.
- [Contributor Covenant 2.1, Enforcement Guidelines](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/): a graded ladder of outcomes (correction, warning, temporary ban, permanent ban), prompt and fair review, and respect for the privacy of the person reporting.
- [Linux Foundation Antitrust Policy](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/legal/antitrust-policy): the topics competitors must not discuss when they meet (prices, bids, customers, markets), and the duty to object when a discussion drifts there.
- [DOJ and FTC Antitrust Policy Statement on Sharing of Cybersecurity Information (2014)](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/atr/legacy/2014/04/10/305027.pdf): the agencies' view that properly designed sharing of cybersecurity information is not likely to raise antitrust concerns, while sharing competitively sensitive information such as prices, costs, and output can. Our allowance for security-focused discussion of compliance practices follows this line.
