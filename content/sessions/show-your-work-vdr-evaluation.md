A scanner finding starts an evaluation. Completing it requires a method for determining likely exploitability, internet reachability, and potential agency impact in the context of the affected service and its agency use.

This session walks through stackArmor's public, deterministic, CVSS-Environmental evaluation method and the evidence behind its decisions. We will examine how FedRAMP's eight evaluation factors inform the three evaluation outputs, including asset security-impact profiles, intended agency use, exploitation signals, reachability, and affected-agency scope. Worked examples will show how the same vulnerability can receive different evaluations in different deployments.

We will explain the assumptions, calibration choices, and tradeoffs in this method, including where another provider could reasonably choose a different approach. Attendees can follow the examples and discuss what additional evidence would change the result. The session gives teams a way to inspect and explain their evaluations, whether they adopt this method or develop their own.

**You will leave with:** Work through contrasting examples; receive the worked evaluations, a reusable decision-record template, and links to the public method and supporting white papers. Participants will have practical questions for assessing the evidence, assumptions, and consistency of their own evaluation method.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with vulnerability findings and basic VDR/VER terminology is helpful. No product account or advance reading is required.
