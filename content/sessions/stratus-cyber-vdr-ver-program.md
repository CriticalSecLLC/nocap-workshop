Every detected vulnerability needs three calls: internet reachability, likely exploitability and potential agency impact. Tools for making those calls get most of the attention. What teams overlook is often the process around them: who approves a rating change, who gets notified, and when a vulnerability should also be handled as a reportable incident.

This session walks a maturity path for both. It starts with the scanners you already run: a side-by-side look at what common scanners provide out of the box, and how to build on them with threat intelligence and simple rules. The next step adds system context (your boundary, data flows and inventory), with AI helping make the call. At the top sits autonomous penetration testing, which checks reachability and exploitability by attempting them; a failed attempt does not by itself make a vulnerability not likely exploitable.

On the process side, you'll see approvals, notifications and escalation running as part of the work, so the record behind every decision exists because the work happened. Examples draw on how we run our own program; all are synthetic.

**You will leave with:** A scanner comparison with ideas for building on the tooling you already have, and a maturity map for VDR/VER evaluation that covers both the tooling and the process around it.
