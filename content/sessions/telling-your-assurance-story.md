Your Federal customers are looking for assurance. What breaks it is data that argues with itself: an incident that names a CVE no vulnerability record mentions, or a transformative change in the quarterly report that no notification explains. That's the biggest red flag there is: nobody can make a risk-based decision on a story that doesn't hold together. And FedRAMP's JSON schemas won't hold it together for you. The only field all six share is a link back to the package overview. The vulnerability record has no field for a CVE or an affected asset. A significant change notification has no identifier of its own. The quarterly report summarizes changes as free text and lists incidents without their tracking identifiers. Every join that makes it coherent is yours to build, before your first report goes out.

In this session, you'll learn how to map how the 2026 JSON artifacts relate, for 20x and Rev5 alike, and the joins you must build. You'll also learn how to hunt for contradictions. Finally, you'll learn how to lay the groundwork: tracking identifiers that stay stable from detection onward, a link from every finding to an asset, a decision record behind every accepted vulnerability, and consistent detection and evaluation times, since CR26 deadlines run from both.

Federal work is built on a foundation of assurance. You'll leave knowing your package's story before your reader does. All examples are synthetic or public. I speak for myself, not FedRAMP or any agency or organization.

**You will leave with:** A map of how FedRAMP's JSON reports connect.
